Why Study Moose-Human Interactions Around Park City?

The greater Park City area provides an ideal setting for studying moose–human interactions because it contains both high-quality moose habitat and high levels of year-round recreation. While most interactions are harmless, conflicts can occur through vehicular collisions, encounters with domestic dogs, and situations where people unknowingly approach moose at close range. As development and outdoor recreation continue to expand across Summit and Wasatch counties, understanding where and when people and moose are most likely to overlap can help inform strategies for reducing conflict.


Why Are Conflicts Escalating Here?

Land development and outdoor recreation continue to expand across Summit and Wasatch counties, creating more opportunities for people and moose to share the same spaces. The area also receives visitors who may be unfamiliar with moose behavior and how to safely share trails and other recreational areas with large wildlife. With the 2034 Winter Olympics approaching, recreation and visitation in the region may continue to change, making it increasingly important to provide the public with information about moose behavior and ways to reduce the risk of conflict.


What Are We Studying?

We are using trail cameras to document when and where moose and recreational trail users overlap on public trails in Summit and Wasatch counties. By examining patterns in moose activity and human recreation, we hope to identify areas and times when the potential for conflict may be greater. This information will be used to develop targeted outreach materials that help recreationists recognize seasonal moose behavior and make informed decisions about sharing the landscape with wildlife. Our goal is to better understand moose–human interactions and use that information to promote safer recreation practices while supporting coexistence between people and moose.
