Why study moose-human interactions around Park City?

The greater Park City area provides an ideal setting for studying moose–human interactions because it contains both high-quality moose habitat and some of the highest levels of year-round recreation in Utah. While most interactions are harmless, conflicts do occur, particularly by way of vehicular collisions, encounters with domestic dogs, and situations where people unknowingly approach moose at close range. In the past several decades, conflicts have been increasing in frequency and severity. We aim to locate hotspots for increased risk of conflict, and provide targeted outreach to the public with the goal of keeping people and moose safer.


Why are conflicts escalating here?

As land development and outdoor recreation continue to skyrocket across Summit and Wasatch counties, encounters with moose are becoming increasingly common. This area also receives a high number of tourists who may be unfamiliar with moose behavior and how to safely share space with large wildlife. With the winter olympics coming in 2034, more people than ever will be recreating in this area, so it is important to provide this information to the public beforehand to help guide management decisions and promote safer recreational practices.
