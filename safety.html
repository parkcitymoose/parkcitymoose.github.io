When Moose Need Extra Space

Moose are not predators, but that doesn’t mean they can’t be dangerous. Most moose would rather avoid a confrontation, but their behavior changes throughout the year. There are two periods when it’s especially important to give them plenty of room.

Calving season: Most calves in Utah are born between May and June. During this period, and in the months following, cows can be extremely protective of their young. If a cow perceives a person or dog as a threat to her calf, she may become defensive or charge. Never approach a calf, even if you don’t immediately see its mother nearby.

The rut: The rut, or moose breeding season, usually runs between September and October. During this time, bull moose experience major behavioral changes as they search for mates and compete with other bulls. They can become more territorial, unpredictable, and aggressive at this time.

Know the Warning Signs

A moose may warn you before becoming aggressive. Watch for behaviors such as:
  • Ears pinned back
  • Hair standing up along the back of their neck
  • Lowering its head
  • Licking its snout

If you notice any of these behaviors, give the moose space and back away immediately. If the moose charges or follows you, get behind a large tree and keep the tree between you and the moose until the animal retreats.
