About the Project

Melany McEntee is a master's student in the Department of Wildland Resources at Utah State University. She obtained her bachelor's degree from California State University, Chico in Ecological, Evolutionary, and Organismal Biology. Prior to studying moose-human interacions in Utah, she worked for several years at a national research reserve, working in both the scientific research department and the visitor's center providing outreach and scientific communication to the public.

David Stoner is a professor in the Department of Wildland Resources at Utah State University. His research addresses questions about the effects of climate and land-use change on valued wildlife species in western ecosystems. To achieve this, he collaborates with state and federal natural resource agencies to develop conservation plans for species that are vulnerable to the effects of light pollution, habitat fragmentation, and exploitation. David's current projects include examining the potential for mountain lions to limit feral horse populations, competitive interactions between feral horses and native ungulates, and the impacts of wildfire and drought on mule deer in the Great Basin.
